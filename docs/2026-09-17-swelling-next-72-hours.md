# Practical swelling plan: September 17–20, 2026

Assistant-prepared decision aid based on reported generalized swelling, approximate weight 64–65 → 66 kg, poor intake with recent increase, and September 17 dexamethasone 16 mg plus Dexalgin 50 mg and nimesulide 100 mg. Not a clinician prescription or confirmed diagnosis. Weight thresholds below are pragmatic action points for this situation, not diagnostic cutoffs. Symptoms and urine output override weight.

## Establish a usable trend

Each morning after urination, before food/drink, use the same scale on a hard floor and similar clothing. Repeat an unexpected reading once. Record weight, visible swelling, approximate drinks and urine, food eaten and medicine names/doses/times. Do not assume fluid intake must equal urine output exactly. Earlier weights were approximate and may not be directly comparable.

## Tomorrow, September 18

- Another approximately 1 kg increase, visibly worse swelling, or clearly reduced urine: same-day in-person fluid/kidney assessment with results available that day. Do not wait for several further kilograms. If an outpatient service cannot examine her and act on results promptly, use an acute medical/emergency department.
- Weight stable, swelling stable/improving, urine usual and otherwise comfortable: home observation is reasonable while obtaining the first repeat laboratory panel within 24 hours. Stable weight alone does not establish normal kidney function or electrolytes.
- Weight decreasing, swelling easing and urine usual: continue observation and small tolerated meals; still obtain the first repeat panel because intake has increased after prolonged restriction.
- Increasing breathlessness, near-absent urine, or recurrence of the already-discussed airway emergency changes the urgency regardless of weight; do not wait for tomorrow's weigh-in.

## What to request

A fluid-status assessment with BP, pulse, oxygen saturation, lung examination, edema distribution/pitting and assessment for excess versus insufficient circulating fluid. Blood: serum creatinine, urea, sodium, potassium, magnesium, phosphate, glucose, albumin. Urinalysis for blood/protein; quantify urine protein if abnormal. Other tests guided by examination rather than blanket imaging.

Ask for comparison against September 14: creatinine 72.3 µmol/L, albumin 39.7 g/L, K 3.66 mmol/L, Mg 0.828 mmol/L, phosphate 1.43 mmol/L, urine ACR 0.4032 mg/mmol. Bring original reports.

Request concrete written decisions: whether medication-related retention is plausible; whether NSAIDs should be stopped; exact Medrol dose/restart/ending plan after dexamethasone; whether thiamine and monitored nutritional increase are indicated; whether any fluid/salt target or diuretic is justified; who will review the results and when to repeat them. Admission is useful if it supplies serial kidney/electrolyte tests, measured urine/fluid balance, monitored treatment or electrolyte correction that cannot be done promptly outside hospital.

## The following 48–72 hours

If weight keeps rising despite initially normal results: repeat clinical assessment and renal/electrolyte tests, rather than relying on September 14 or one new normal panel. If low intake and refeeding risk persist, phosphate/Mg/K may need daily monitoring initially. Increasing creatinine, abnormal potassium/phosphate/Mg, substantial urine protein or persistently low urine require an actionable same-day review; critical results or inability to obtain prompt treatment favor hospital care.

If weight remains stable and swelling gradually improves with reassuring results and usual urine, continue home observation. Arrange a targeted reassessment if significant swelling remains unchanged after 48–72 hours. Consider cardiac testing only if examination/symptoms support it; renal specialist input if kidney/urine abnormalities emerge. C4/C1-inhibitor testing addresses recurrent attacks separately and does not substitute for this fluid assessment.

Do not independently start diuretics, sharply restrict fluids, force water or add salt/electrolyte supplements. Do not use extra steroids to chase body swelling or repeat NSAIDs while the current swelling/renal uncertainty is unresolved. Continue small tolerated meals rather than fasting or a large catch-up meal. Confirm the steroid plan before the next scheduled dose; this document does not establish a multi-day steroid stop.

Recent systemic steroid exposure is documented in the history; exact doses and symptom timing at this blood draw are not supplied. This is the September 19 order, not proof that the separately planned September 24 tryptase draw occurred.

The August 29 original was visually rechecked: **11.6 µg/L**, same ImmunoCAP method and reference <11, collection approximately **03:05**. Dmytro previously reported collection **5–6 hours after symptom onset**.

Using 8.44 provisionally as baseline, the consensus acute-rise threshold is **1.2 × 8.44 + 2 = 12.128 µg/L (about 12.13)**. The August value 11.6 does not meet that threshold. This pair therefore does not demonstrate the consensus tryptase rise. It also does not exclude anaphylaxis: the earlier sample was late and may have missed the peak. Do not reconstruct an unmeasured peak or label the small shortfall diagnostic. A normal baseline alone neither diagnoses nor excludes MCAS. [AAAAI practice-parameter guide](https://www.aaaai.org/Aaaai/media/Media-Library-PDFs/Allergist%20Resources/Statements%20and%20Practice%20Parameters/Anaphylaxis-in-Practice-2023.pdf); [WAO guidance](https://www.worldallergyorganizationjournal.org/article/S1939-4551%2820%2930375-6/fulltext).

For a more interpretable comparison, baseline timing is at least 24 hours after resolution of an acute systemic reaction, while an acute sample is ideally obtained within two hours of onset. This is guidance for interpreting/testing, not evidence another test has occurred. If the current sample was collected during ongoing/recent systemic symptoms, retain it as provisional. ECNM/AIM experts also note that a basal level may reasonably be rechecked after corticosteroids have been discontinued when clinically appropriate; no fixed washout is established here and no steroid interruption is advised. [ECNM/AIM consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11869997/).

A secondary nuance: 8.44 is just above the >8 ng/mL threshold at which the AAAAI guide suggests considering hereditary alpha-tryptasemia in a person with recurrent/severe anaphylaxis (µg/L and ng/mL are numerically equivalent). This is not an abnormal laboratory flag or a genetic diagnosis. If a stable baseline remains above 8, TPSAB1 copy-number testing is a possible later investigation; this single result does not establish that testing is needed or accessible. No genetic testing or new diagnosis is recorded as completed. Normal-range tryptase also does not by itself exclude a clonal mast-cell disorder. [AAAAI guide](https://www.aaaai.org/Aaaai/media/Media-Library-PDFs/Allergist%20Resources/Statements%20and%20Practice%20Parameters/Anaphylaxis-in-Practice-2023.pdf).

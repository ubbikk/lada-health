# Review of Lada’s September 14 prescriptions

Prepared September 14, 2026. Independent medication review, not a new prescription or confirmation of actual use. The Ukrainian daily sheet is in [Word](2026-09-14-lada-medication-schedule.docx). The sample times organize the written doses; unresolved choices are explicitly excluded from an automatic routine.

## Overall assessment

There is a sensible core: an asthma controller, treatment for nose/eye/skin symptoms, and a trial of acid suppression for upper abdominal symptoms. The problem is incomplete reconciliation between prescribers, unclear device doses, overlapping oral antihistamines, and a particularly questionable duration of domperidone. These papers do not establish the cause of the repeated systemic reactions, poor intake or swelling. The gastroenterologist explicitly leaves active gastritis, H. pylori and small-intestinal bacterial overgrowth uncertain.

## What matters most

1. **EpiPen availability:** highest consequence if unavailable during anaphylaxis. It is rescue treatment, not a daily preventive medicine. Keep two usable devices accessible. The paper’s 30-minute repeat interval is unsafe to rely on: repeat IM adrenaline after **5 minutes** when serious airway/breathing/circulation symptoms persist. Antihistamines and steroids do not replace it. [Resuscitation Council UK](https://www.resus.org.uk/media/337/download).
2. **Seretide every day:** the main maintenance asthma medicine here, containing fluticasone and salmeterol. Regular use and correct technique matter more than adding further symptom tablets. The prescription says “one dose” twice daily, without defining puffs. The product’s usual adult regimen is two puffs twice daily, but that does not authorize changing her individualized dose. Obtain the intended puff count and confirm technique; rinse and spit afterward. It is not a rescue inhaler, and a separate rapid-relief asthma instruction is missing from these sheets. [GSK product information](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/7599/smpc).
3. **A consistent, tolerable antihistamine plan:** useful for ongoing itching, hives and rhinitis; not guaranteed prevention of severe anaphylaxis. Nixar is bilastine. The paper specifies a 10 mg tablet morning and evening, total 20 mg/day. Standard adult bilastine is 20 mg once daily; asking whether that simpler schedule is intended is reasonable, but the handout retains the written split dose. Take at least one hour before or two hours after food/fruit juice. [Bilastine leaflet](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/4551/pil).
4. **Medrol dosing and an explicit ending plan:** adherence matters because steroid exposure is already complex. Written dose is methylprednisolone 16 mg after breakfast plus 8 mg after the evening meal for September 14–20. Dmytro previously confirmed it replaces dexamethasone. Do not stack the two regimens or implement the ambiguous optional increase. Steroids may be appropriate for an asthma flare or another inflammatory indication, but routine use to prevent recurrent/biphasic anaphylaxis is not evidence-based. An isolated course shorter than 3–4 weeks generally does not require tapering; her repeated recent exposure means we cannot apply that shortcut or design a taper without the actual dose history. [RCUK](https://www.resus.org.uk/media/337/download), [Endocrine Society guideline](https://www.endocrine.org/clinical-practice-guidelines/glucocorticoid-induced-adrenal-insufficiency).

## Items to resolve or reconsider

**Quamatel is duplicated.** Both prescriptions are famotidine: allergist 20 mg twice daily for seven days; gastroenterologist 40 mg at bedtime for fourteen days. Each totals 40 mg/day; adding them gives 80 mg/day, which is not established as intended. The later prescription may replace the earlier one, but that is unconfirmed. Choose one explicitly reconciled schedule. Famotidine can generally be taken with or without food, so its empty-stomach requirement is less important than the bilastine timing. [Famotidine product information](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/15973/smpc).

**Domrid SR is the strongest prescribing concern.** The written 30 mg once daily for 28 days conflicts with the current Ukrainian manufacturer leaflet’s maximum one-week duration. It can help nausea, but prolonged low intake makes electrolyte assessment particularly relevant. Before starting, obtain a practical review of ECG/QTc, potassium, magnesium and interacting medicines. Prior resolved obstruction is not an automatic contraindication; current mechanical obstruction is. Avoid automatically adding this course to the pill routine. If ultimately selected, the SR tablet should remain whole, and its timing must be reconciled with acid-suppressing drugs. [Ukrainian manufacturer leaflet, revised December 25, 2025](https://kusumukraine.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/instruction-domrid-sr-tablets-ua.pdf).

**Glencet Advance overlaps with Nixar.** It already contains levocetirizine 5 mg plus montelukast 10 mg. Levocetirizine can cause sleepiness/fatigue, relevant to Lada’s symptoms, although it does not prove the cause. Montelukast is an add-on option, not a replacement for inhaled steroid treatment. New nightmares, marked mood/behaviour changes or suicidal thoughts require stopping montelukast and prompt medical contact. These risks deserve an explicit benefit review. [Manufacturer](https://glenmarkpharma.ua/products/respiratornyy-napryamok/retsepturni/hlentset-edvans), [FDA warning](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-requires-boxed-warning-about-serious-mental-health-side-effects-asthma-and-allergy-drug).

For chronic urticaria, current guidance favors increasing one second-generation H1 antihistamine when necessary over mixing different ones. That evidence does not diagnose Lada with chronic urticaria or dictate treatment of her systemic episodes, but it supports a focused simplification discussion: what measurable benefit requires both oral H1 drugs? Do not independently double the Nixar or add old Suprastin/Rupafin/Cetrin regimens. [2026 international urticaria guideline](https://doi.org/10.1111/all.70210).

**Omez is reasonable symptom treatment, with a testing conflict.** Omeprazole 20 mg 30 minutes before breakfast for fourteen days is a conventional acid-suppression trial. It does not eradicate H. pylori or confirm gastritis. Routine addition of bedtime famotidine is not automatically necessary; it needs a symptom-based reason. If H. pylori stool antigen has not been collected, determine the testing sequence before initiating Omez. PPIs can cause false-negative results; a two-week PPI washout is usual, while her lab order requests three weeks. Recent antibiotics/bismuth generally require four weeks. H2 blockers may bridge the interval, subject to the particular laboratory’s final-dose instructions. Do not compromise symptom treatment for weeks without settling this sequence. [ACG guidance](https://gi.org/journals-publications/ebgi/schoenfeld_sep2024/).

**Ryaltris and Opatanol have reasonable targeted roles.** Nasal olopatadine/mometasone treats rhinitis; eye olopatadine treats allergic eye symptoms. Their different delivery sites are not automatically wasteful duplication. They are lower priority for preventing severe outcomes, but relief may materially improve daily function. Ryaltris’s “one dose” needs a per-nostril spray count; do not silently substitute the usual adult label dose of two sprays per nostril twice daily. Opatanol is one drop in affected eye(s) twice daily, approximately eight hours apart. Do not automatically continue old Maxidex or Okomistin schedules alongside it. [Ryaltris product information](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/12898/smpc), [Opatanol EMA information](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/opatanol).

## Diet and the unresolved nutrition problem

The generic diet sheet should not become a rigid food blacklist. It recommends foods that are not established safe for her personally, and the consultation’s “stone fruits” category oddly lists persimmon, pomegranate and grapes. None of this identifies her actual allergens. Avoid suspected reaction triggers while preserving individually tolerated foods; cooking is not a blanket guarantee of allergen safety. General gastritis guidance does not justify extensive restrictions for everyone. [NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/gastritis-gastropathy/eating-diet-nutrition).

The unexplained **up-to-1.2 L/day fluid restriction** deserves clarification in the context of reported 90/60 BP and very low intake; these photographs provide no individualized fluid-balance rationale. Swelling alone does not establish a need for that limit. I would not turn it into an automatic compliance target.

The important omission is a concrete plan for weeks of minimal nutrition. Blood count, iron/vitamin tests and generic diet advice do not cover safe restoration of intake. First establish whether today's chemistry includes **potassium, magnesium, phosphate, glucose and serum renal function**, and whether a thiamine/feeding-monitoring plan was supplied verbally. These are practical gaps, not diagnoses or instructions to self-dose electrolytes. [NICE nutrition guidance](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg32/chapter/recommendations).

## Making adherence easier

Ask the clinic for a short written correction covering the single Quamatel regimen, the device doses, the steroid ending plan and Domrid duration. A pharmacist can check products, tablets and interactions, while the prescriber resolves therapeutic intent. No need to reconstruct the entire case merely to obtain those concrete corrections.

# Test shortlist for Lada — September 14, 2026

This is our working shortlist, separate from the doctor handouts. It is based on the history reported through September 13, not an examination or a set of clinician orders. The immediate question is what could change treatment or the admission decision. Check any results obtained tomorrow before paying for duplicates.

## 1. Highest priority: current bloodwork and urine

I would prioritize this group even if the eventual explanation for the attacks remains uncertain:

| Test | Why it is useful here |
|---|---|
| CBC with differential and red-cell indices | Recheck the August hemoglobin of 111 g/L and low MCV, and white-cell abnormalities; substantial past bleeding and low intake make this relevant. |
| Sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate/total CO₂; urea and creatinine | Current electrolytes and kidney function. The old potassium result was high, not low; it needs a fresh measurement rather than an assumption of deficiency. |
| **Phosphate and magnesium — explicitly included** | Prolonged minimal intake and recent increases in eating make these particularly relevant; ordinary chemistry packages often omit them. |
| Glucose | Useful with severe fatigue/sleepiness, low intake and steroid exposure; a measurement during symptoms may be more informative than an unrelated fasting result. |
| Calcium with albumin; consider repeat ionized calcium | Prior ionized calcium was low. |
| Albumin, total protein, ALT, AST, ALP, bilirubin | Help evaluate generalized swelling and liver/protein abnormalities. Albumin alone does not diagnose malnutrition. |
| Urinalysis plus a spot urine protein:creatinine or albumin:creatinine ratio | Look for protein loss through the kidneys in the setting of marked swelling. Choose the ratio with the assessing service; routinely ordering both is unnecessary. |
| Ferritin, transferrin saturation (iron plus transferrin/TIBC), CRP | Assess iron deficiency and help interpret ferritin after recent illness. Iron alone is insufficient. |

The electrolyte, glucose and nutritional-monitoring priorities follow [NICE nutrition-support guidance](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg32/chapter/Recommendations). The swelling workup is consistent with the [Merck Manual approach to generalized edema](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/cardiovascular-disorders/symptoms-of-cardiovascular-disorders/edema). The iron studies follow [BSG guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8515119/).

A single normal set of electrolytes does not establish that increasing food intake will remain uncomplicated. With little or no intake for more than ten days, NICE identifies high refeeding risk regardless of BMI; her exact intake remains an estimate. If this applies, potassium, phosphate, magnesium, glucose and fluid balance need a defined monitoring plan as intake increases. This is a reason to assess whether hospital-based nutritional support is needed, not to keep her underfed. [NICE](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg32/chapter/Recommendations)

## 2. Abdominal assessment may change what should happen first

The March obstruction and multiple subsequent pelvic operations with reported adhesions mean blood tests alone cannot settle the abdominal question. An abdominal examination and assessment of bowel function should guide imaging. If obstruction is suspected, CT can identify its location, severity and cause; an ultrasound or normal bloodwork does not by itself exclude it. Adhesions are a possibility, not an established explanation for all her symptoms. [NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/abdominal-adhesions)

Gastroscopy is a reasonable discussion given persistent pain/nausea, extremely limited eating and the childhood ulcer history, but the examination should determine urgency and sequencing. It will not substitute for assessing possible obstruction. Pelvic examination/ultrasound is conditional on pain location, current bleeding and findings, given her repeated cyst complications.

## 3. Allergy investigations worth pursuing

- **Baseline serum tryptase:** high priority in recurrent severe/unexplained reactions. Her 11.6 µg/L result was taken during an episode, approximately 5–6 hours after onset; it is not a baseline. Obtain a baseline more than 24 hours after acute symptoms have resolved. If she is still symptomatic when blood is taken, record this and do not automatically label that sample baseline. During a new suspected anaphylaxis, an acute sample ideally within 1–2 hours is useful for comparison; sampling must not delay treatment. [AAAAI/ACAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/Aaaai/media/Media-Library-PDFs/Allergist%20Resources/Statements%20and%20Practice%20Parameters/Anaphylaxis-Practice-Paramaters-2023.pdf), [NHS laboratory timing](https://www.nwlpathology.nhs.uk/test/tryptase-mast-cell-tryptase-serum/)
- **Targeted specific IgE:** select foods/exposures from the episode history, potentially mango, shrimp, peach, milk and fish; the new egg event also warrants review. These are candidates to evaluate, not a confirmed-allergy list. Relevant pollen testing may help explain the seasonal symptoms. A positive result indicates sensitization and must be matched to the history; broad panels can create misleading food exclusions. [ASCIA](https://allergy.org.au/hp/papers/tests-in-the-diagnosis-of-allergic-diseases)
- **Timing of ALEX/IgE:** the September 9 prescription specifically requests ALEX 14 days after the last dexamethasone. This does not justify delaying the general bloodwork above. Blood specific-IgE testing differs from skin testing: antihistamines do not interfere with serum specific IgE. Do not stop necessary medicines simply to arrange tests; the allergist should specify the rationale and timing for the chosen test. [ASCIA clinical update](https://www.allergy.org.au/images/stories/pospapers/ar/ASCIA_HP_Clinical_Update_Allergic_Rhinitis_2024.pdf)
- **C4 plus C1-inhibitor level and function:** a conditional addition if the history suggests episodes of swelling without hives/itching or inadequate response to appropriate treatment. Recurrent swelling plus abdominal attacks makes it worth explicitly considering; her prominent itching/rash also points to other mechanisms. C4 alone is not a complete evaluation. [WAO guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13184495/)

## 4. Useful gastrointestinal tests, with timing limitations

- **H. pylori stool antigen or urea breath test:** reasonable given ulcer/gastritis history and ongoing upper-abdominal symptoms. PPIs can cause false negatives; NICE specifies a two-week washout. Recent antibiotics/bismuth also affect timing: check the laboratory protocol and actual medication history before collection, rather than stopping treatment independently. [NICE dyspepsia guidance](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg184/chapter/Recommendations)
- **Coeliac screen: total IgA and tTG-IgA:** reasonable with persistent GI symptoms and possible iron deficiency. Very low gluten intake can make a negative result unreliable. Record her recent diet; do not introduce a home gluten challenge while eating is this difficult and reactions remain unresolved. [NICE coeliac guidance](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng20/chapter/Recommendations)
- **Faecal calprotectin:** conditional on persistent lower-bowel symptoms/diarrhea and concern for intestinal inflammation. It is not a test for obstruction and should not postpone necessary imaging or endoscopy. [NICE](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/htg320)

## What I would not buy as an initial package

Food-IgG “intolerance” panels are not supported for diagnosing food allergy or intolerance. [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/igg-food-test)

I would also avoid an indiscriminate collection of genetic/mast-cell tests before baseline tryptase and clinical assessment have narrowed the question. Further investigation can be appropriate in recurrent unexplained anaphylaxis, even with a normal baseline; that does not make every such test a useful first purchase. [AAAAI/ACAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/Aaaai/media/Media-Library-PDFs/Allergist%20Resources/Statements%20and%20Practice%20Parameters/Anaphylaxis-Practice-Paramaters-2023.pdf)
